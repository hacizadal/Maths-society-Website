When Math Meets Cinema: Interstellar

Exploring the physics and mathematics behind Christopher Nolan's masterpiece

Watch the Full Seminar

Presented by Pratikeet (Freshman) and Tashar (Second Year)
Math Society Topic Tutorial Series - Episode 2

About the Movie

Interstellar (2014) is a science fiction masterpiece directed by Christopher Nolan. The film features Matthew McConaughey as Cooper, a NASA engineer and pilot who attempts to save dying humanity by finding a new planet through a wormhole near Saturn.

What makes Interstellar unique is its collaboration with Kip Thorne, a Nobel Prize-winning physicist who served as the scientific advisor. Thorne derived formulas specifically for the visual effects and helped create the most accurate black hole visualization ever seen in cinema—Gargantua.

Hans Zimmer's iconic score features ticking at 1.25-second intervals, where each tick represents one day passing on Earth—a subtle detail that emphasizes the time dilation theme.

Main Questions Explored

Question 1: How Far is Miller's Planet from Gargantua?

Miller's planet experiences extreme time dilation where 1 hour on the planet equals 7 years on Earth. The planet also features massive tidal waves 1.2 km high and has 130% of Earth's gravity.

The Mathematics

Using tidal acceleration and energy conservation, we can derive the distance:

Tidal acceleration: a ≈ (G·M·y) / d³
Where G = Newton's constant, M = Mass of Gargantua, d = Distance

After applying the work-energy theorem and integrating using the Mean Value Theorem:

d = ∛(3·M·R⁴ / (2·m·H))

Result: 4.5 billion km from Gargantua's center

The Problem: Time Dilation Inconsistency

The movie claims a time dilation factor of 6,000 (1 hour = 7 years).
However, using the actual physics equations with the calculated distance, the time dilation factor is only 1.05.

Conclusion: The solution is NOT scientifically valid. This is where science fiction takes over from science fact.

Question 2: Why Wasn't Cooper Spaghettified?

Brane Cosmology Theory

Branes (short for "membrane") are a concept from string theory. Our universe exists as the middle layer between two "confining branes" that allow interaction with higher dimensions.

What Happens to Cooper:

  1. Falls through Gargantua (the black hole)
  2. Transferred from our brane to a confining brane
  3. Enters a 5-dimensional tesseract
  4. Can interact with different points in spacetime simultaneously

Einstein's Field Equations

The mathematics behind spacetime curvature:

R_μν - (1/2)g_μν·R = Λ·g_μν

Left side: Ricci curvature tensor (describes local spacetime curvature)
Right side: Metric tensor (describes geometry)
Λ: Cosmological constant (determines universe shape)

De Sitter vs. Anti-de Sitter Spaces

De Sitter Space (Positive Curvature)

f(r) = 1/√(1 - α²·r²)

Distances are enlarged. Space curves outward like a sphere.

Anti-de Sitter Space (Negative Curvature)

f(r) = √(1 + r²/R²)

Distances are crumpled/contracted. Space curves inward like a saddle.
This is what Cooper experiences in Gargantua.

Distance Crumpling Effect

In our brane (normal space): Gargantua to Earth = 10 billion light-years
In the confining brane (AdS space): Distance crumpled to = 1 AU (150 million km)

This is a compression ratio of approximately 67,000,000:1! This explains how Cooper can send gravitational waves from inside Gargantua back to Earth through the tesseract.

Problems with the Movie's Physics

1. Instantaneous Communication

Light takes 8.3 minutes to travel 1 AU. Even with crumpled space, Cooper's messages to Murph appear instantaneous, violating known physics.

2. Quantum Entanglement Doesn't Help

The No-Communication Theorem states that two quantum-entangled observers cannot exchange information instantaneously, even if gravitational waves were quantum in nature.

3. No Spaghettification

Given our current understanding of black holes, Cooper should have been torn apart by tidal forces. The movie doesn't explain why he wasn't.

4. Brane Instability

If confining branes keep pressing on our universe, eventually the branes would buckle and the universe would collapse. The model has inherent stability problems.

Key Takeaways

1. Appreciate the Effort: While not 100% accurate, Interstellar represents one of the most scientifically grounded sci-fi films ever made.

2. Motivation for Learning: The inconsistencies should inspire us to explore unexplored frontiers of physics and push the boundaries of what's possible.

3. Real Achievement: The movie's visualization of Gargantua was so accurate that when the first real black hole image was captured, it looked remarkably similar!

4. Science Fiction = Science + Fiction: It's okay for movies to take liberties—that's what makes them inspiring and entertaining.
